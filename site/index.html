How it works

The current counts.
The microphone listens.

A MIG arc heats the joint as a wire feeds into it. On a repeat job, the fixture and the job recipe tell Nabz how many arcs to expect and how long each should last. Miller Electric ↗

  1. 01 / Sense

    Find each arc

    A proposed clamp on the welding cable measures current. On/off thresholds and a minimum dwell define the arc window. A confirmed fixture or operator event closes the part cycle.

  2. 02 / Compare

    Check the job recipe

    Count arc windows and check their duration. Score phone audio inside those windows against inspected good welds from the same job. A change in wire, gas, or recipe calls for a new baseline.

  3. 03 / Respond

    Tell the operator why

    A missing or short arc means hold the part for inspection. Sustained sound drift earns a setup check. If a sensor drops out or the cycle boundary is uncertain, show that status and use the inspection plan.

What the signals establish. Current shows when an arc fired and for how long. A correct count can still occur at the wrong location, and a short arc only suggests a short weld. Sound drift is an advisory. Weld geometry, strength and acceptance still come from inspection.
